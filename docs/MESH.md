# Meshes (STL): a short guide

A mesh is a set of triangles from an STL file (binary or ASCII). It is not converted to a solid: it stays a mesh, and the edits are made on the triangles (see D60 in `DECISIONS.md`).

## In `rolidium cli` and MCP

* `import-stl part.stl [inch | scale k] [weld]`: STL has no units; the numbers are millimetres, or inches, or `scale k` (one unit is k mm). The answer gives the number of triangles, the size (bounding box) and the check.
* `mesh-check`: open edges (holes), edges where the normals disagree, edges of more than two triangles, degenerate triangles, loose pieces, a mesh turned inside out. `mesh-repair` joins duplicate vertices and turns the triangles so that neighbours (and the whole piece) agree about the outside. Holes are not filled.
* `mesh-regions`: the planar walls (`wall #1`, ...) and the round holes and pins (`hole #1`, ...).
* `mesh-pull <wall> <distance>`: the wall moves along its normal and the neighbouring walls stretch; it works when the neighbours are square to the wall (boxes, plates, brackets). Refused with the reason otherwise; the mesh stays as it was. `mesh-hole <hole> <diameter>` changes the diameter of a round hole or pin. `mesh-undo` and `mesh-redo` take back one edit each (only the moved vertices are remembered).
* `mesh-scale sx sy sz` (uneven allowed), `mesh-move`, `mesh-rotate`, `mesh-mirror`, `mesh-pattern`, `export-mesh-stl`, `mesh-save` and `mesh-open` (the compact form: a mesh of a printer is saved in a small part of the size of the STL).
* The commands work on the last mesh, or on `mesh N`.

Examples with their own geometry (CC0) are in `examples/stl/`: `import-stl examples/stl/plate-with-hole.stl`, `mesh-regions`, `mesh-pull 1 5`, `mesh-hole 1 6`.

## In the window

* **Import**: the button **Import STL or 3MF** in the File block, or drop one or several `.stl` or `.3mf` files into the window (a 3MF file gives a mesh for every object of its build, with its own unit; colours, materials and print settings are ignored). A dialog lists the files with the number of triangles, the size in millimetres (it follows the choice of the units: millimetres, inches, or a scale of your own, "1 unit of the file is ... mm"), and the result of the check; the check says in plain words what it found, and a button is there only for a problem it mends: **Join the loose joints** (points that lie next to each other were not joined) and **Turn them outwards** (triangles that face inwards); edges shared by more than two faces (two parts that touch along an edge) and holes in the surface are named, with the note that the buttons do not repair them. A repair says in one sentence what it did. **Import** adds the meshes to the document; **Cancel** (or Esc) adds nothing.
* **A mesh in the document** is a body of its own kind (`▦` in the list of bodies with its number of triangles): select it, hide it, rename it (F2), put it into a group, delete it, Ctrl+Z. It is drawn by the same renderer with the flat shading of the triangles, and picked on the processor through a hierarchy of boxes (the same as for solids).
* **Walls and holes** are looked for in the background after the import (a small window shows the progress and a Cancel button; the window keeps drawing and answering). Until they are found the mesh is one piece (a click takes the whole mesh); when they are found, the pointer over a wall or a hole lights it, a click selects it, and the panel next to it shows what it is: for a flat wall the area and the distance to the opposite wall, for a hole or a pin the diameter. A surface that is only flat by chance (a finely divided ball, a scanned or sculpted model) has no walls: a patch is a wall when a crease runs round most of it and it is not smaller than a ten-thousandth of the whole surface.
* **Round surfaces**: the strips of a coarse cylinder (8 to 24 round, a part of a circle too) are one surface with a diameter and an axis (the panel shows both; the diameter is changed like that of a hole and the surface stays selected; its flat end is pulled along the axis). Not found: cones (the strips of a cone are walls).
* **Pull (P)** a flat wall: live preview, a number can be typed (Enter), or the mouse drags; the same as for solids (and also the field "Move by" and the distance to the opposite wall in the panel). The neighbouring walls stretch. A hole or a pin: its diameter in the panel (the field, Enter). A wall that cannot be moved (a neighbour that is not square to it, a mesh with holes, a pull through the opposite wall, a mesh that would cut through itself) is shown as such while it is dragged (a red outline, no prism, the reason at the cursor) and refused with the reason on the status line; the mesh does not change. **Move anyway, the neighbouring walls slant** (a tick in the panel of the wall) moves a wall whose neighbours slope: the triangles next to it are stretched, after a check that the mesh does not cut through itself; the walls are then looked for again.
* **The sizes of the body**: with a mesh or a solid selected as a body, X, Y and Z of its box are drawn round it; a click on one opens it for typing. A flat wall at the end is moved (the end at the negative side stays; "Symmetric about the centre"), else the body is scaled in that direction (circles become ellipses; "Keep the proportions" scales all three); several bodies are scaled as a whole. The setting "Show the size of a selected body" turns it off.
* **The whole mesh**: Move (M: X, Y, Z, an angle, Copy), Pattern (T) and Mirror (B) make plain copies (no link that follows the original); **Scale** (factors for x, y and z, also uneven) and the check with its repairs are in the Selection panel in the right column. **Sketch (K)**, or a drawing tool chosen in the 3D view (`R`, `C`, `A`, `X`, `Shift+L`, or its button: click the wall, the first point is where you clicked), draws in the plane of a flat wall (with snaps to its outline); what is pulled from the sketch is a new solid.
* **Convert to a solid** (Selection panel of a mesh, or "Import as: solid" in the import dialog): the mesh becomes a B-rep body (D63, D65): every tool works on it. **Flat and round faces are recognised**: planes, cylinders (holes, pins, rounds along a straight edge), cones (countersinks, chamfers of holes, tapered bosses), spheres (the corner where rounds meet) and tori (a round along a circular edge, the rounded corner of a pocket); the boundaries between neighbouring faces are lines, circles and ellipses fitted to the points of the mesh, so a hole is a real circle and its diameter, a round's radius and a wall with rounds next to it are changed like on a drawn solid. The points of a mesh from a CAD program lie on the real surface (only the facets lie inside it), so round surfaces are fitted to the points and held to a tighter tolerance than flat ones; the volume of the solid is that of the true surface (a coarse hole of 24 strips gives a little more volume than the mesh has). What is not recognised stays flat faces (single triangles). The panel of the solid says the faces by kind, the share of the surface in round faces and in single triangles, the largest deviation, the volume against the mesh and the time. Accuracy (default 0.02 mm), "Round the sizes", "Keep the original mesh, hidden". Small flat holes of the mesh are patched first (and said), loose joints are mended on a copy, a big hole with a curved rim stops the conversion with the reason, so do edges of more than two faces. If the kernel does not accept the result with all kinds of surfaces, fewer kinds are tried, and at the last the flat conversion. One step of Undo brings the mesh back. Big and organic meshes are better left as meshes (the dialog advises).
* **Cut and join with meshes** (0.3.2): Combine (merge, cut, intersect) works for meshes and solids in any combination, and a sketch pulled in the mode Cut cuts through a mesh, Add and Auto join the new volume to a mesh it touches or overlaps. The library is Manifold (Apache-2.0, in `third_party/manifold`, compiled into the program, no shared library; D66). A solid that takes part is meshed first (fine: a few ten-thousandths of its size), so the result is a mesh. The operation runs in the background, Esc stops it, one step of Undo brings everything back. Before the operation **small flat holes of a mesh are patched** (a rim in one plane, any size; a rim that is only nearly flat and wider than 10 mm is not) and the status line says how many; a hole that is left stops the operation with the reason ("its rim is curved (0.8 mm off a plane) and 42 mm wide; filling it would invent a surface").
* **Simplify the mesh** (Selection panel of a mesh, the header *Simplify the mesh*): the target is a share of the triangles or their number; *Keep sharp edges and the rims of holes* (on by default) keeps sharp edges (over 30 degrees), borders and the rims of holes where they are, and a flat face loses almost all its triangles. *Show the result* calculates in the background (Stop) and shows the result **next to the original** in the same view (rotate and zoom as usual); the panel gives the triangles before and after, the largest and the mean deviation of the original's points from the new surface, and the time. When the target cannot be reached the mesh is simplified as far as it can be and the window says why. *Apply the simplification* makes the result the mesh, one step of Undo brings the original back. Edge collapse with quadric errors (D67); 500 000 triangles to 10 % take about 3 s.
* **Stretch across a plane** (Selection panel of a mesh or a solid, the header *Stretch across a plane*): the plane is X, Y or Z with a position, or a click on a flat face; a distance in millimetres. The part on the side of the normal moves by the distance and the gap is filled with the cross-section, swept the same way: a box grows through its flat walls, and holes, rounds, pockets and the rounded corners on each side keep their size and only move. For a mesh it also works on organic shapes (the cross-section is whatever the plane cuts); *Show the result* draws the stretched mesh in place of the original; a mesh must be closed along the plane. A negative distance takes out a layer when the cross-section is the same along it. For a solid the lower part is pulled by the face in the plane and the upper part is moved and fused (D68). One step of Undo.
* **Not possible yet** (the status line says so in one sentence): a round or a chamfer on a mesh, joining a mesh with a solid or cutting it by a solid (a mesh is not a solid: convert it to a solid first, D63; booleans on meshes are not done), a mirror image merged with its original, a pattern of a mesh together with a solid.
* **Save and open**: a `.rolidium` file with a mesh is format version 2 (`FILE-FORMAT.md`), the mesh in a compact form; an older program refuses it with a clear sentence. A document with meshes and solids together exports STL as one file (the triangles of the mesh as they are, the solids divided as set in the dialog); with the choice **Only the selected bodies** in the dialog only those.
* In `rolidium cli`: `mesh-to-doc` makes a mesh of the script a body of the document (saved in `.rolidium`, listed by `info`, exported by `export-stl`).

The numbers measured for a mesh of 2 million triangles in the window (reading and the check 2 to 4 s, walls and holes found in the background in 5 to 7 s, a pick under the pointer 0.03 to 0.07 ms, a pull of a wall in seconds with the redraw of a software renderer) are in `DECISIONS.md` (D61). `cargo run --release -p rolidium-mesh --example make_big -- sphere 2000000 ball.stl` and `... -- holes 32 32 320 plate.stl` make the two meshes of the tests, and `cargo run --release -p rolidium-doc --example stl_dir -- <directory>` tries every STL file of a directory (reading, the check, the walls and holes, the saved size).

## Round surfaces of a converted mesh: what is recognised and what is not

Tried on models made for the tests (`crates/rolidium-doc/tests/mesh_roundtrip.rs`: a solid is meshed like an STL file, converted, and compared): a box with all edges rounded (6 planes, 12 cylinders, 8 spheres come back exactly, the radii within 0.005 mm), a plate with a through hole, a counterbore, a countersink and a chamfer (11 planes, 4 cylinders and 1 cone, the same), a boss with a cone on top and a round at its foot (a torus), a container with rounded corners, a pocket and four holes for magnets. Not recognised well: **tori with a small major radius** (a round at the bottom of a pocket whose corner radius is not much bigger than the round: the "spindle" torus): those pieces stay flat strips, and the rest of the part is still made of real faces. Also a face of fewer than six triangles is not looked at, and a round wall of a mesh is not yet the axis of a circular pattern. The tools that change a diameter or a radius need a *whole* hole or round; a hole that is split into pieces by the mesh (not welded) is not.
